# Executive Memo

**Directed To:** Programme Operations Lead

The biggest pattern from this analysis was that many fellows who did not complete the programme slowly became less engaged before dropping out completely. In many cases, reduced reflection survey participation appeared several weeks earlier, making disengagement easier to identify ahead of time.

To identify this pattern, the analysis focused on fellows who:
- had been enrolled in the programme for more than three weeks,
- still had an incomplete completion status,
- and had not submitted a reflection survey response within the last two weeks.

The shorter enrollment threshold was intentionally used to support earlier disengagement detection and faster operational intervention rather than waiting until fellows had already disengaged completely.

The analysis showed a clear difference between fellows who completed the programme and those who did not. Fellows who completed the programme generally participated more consistently in weekly reflection surveys, while fellows who eventually dropped out slowly stopped responding over time. This suggests that reflection survey participation can be used as an early warning sign for disengagement.

## Recommendations

- Introduce an email automated early-warning outreach process for fellows who stop responding to weekly reflection surveys while still remaining incomplete. Earlier follow-up from programme coordinators could help re-engage fellows before they fully disengage from the programme.

- Standardize data collection across all ALCs to improve reporting consistency and reduce data quality issues. Cleaner and more structured data would make future analysis faster, easier, and more reliable across the programme.

## Data Quality Challenges

The analysis identified several data quality issues in the source files, including:
- duplicate records,
- inconsistent phone number formats,
- mixed-case state names,
- missing ALC codes,
- and structural formatting inconsistencies.

These issues were cleaned and standardized before analysis. However, some missing ALC values could not be confidently identified and were therefore left as null values to avoid unreliable assumptions.

## Limitation

The available datasets mainly focused on operational participation and engagement activity. The data did not include information about learner satisfaction, internet access, financial challenges, or facilitator quality, all of which may also affect programme completion outcomes.

## Conclusion

Overall, the findings suggest that earlier engagement monitoring, faster intervention, and more standardized operational reporting could improve completion and certification outcomes across the programme.
